These are separately frozen operating rules, not a license to select a new objective after inspecting test outcomes. In particular, CV_F2's larger test F1 does not establish it as the prospectively best threshold. Report all prespecified rules and distinguish model selection from threshold selection.

Additional observations that constrain the story:

1. HET_TOP_10 has ROC-AUC **0.999274** but average precision **0.624701**. Its paper threshold has FPR **18.1676%**; a high AUC is not an operational alarm guarantee.
2. The selected homogeneous OCSVM pair has ROC-AUC **0.999900** and average precision **0.771821**, above those heterogeneous figures. Do not claim heterogeneous ensembles universally win.
3. OCSVM_PCA and the selected homogeneous pair detect **0/119** at their frozen CV_F1 thresholds. This is evidence of unsuccessful threshold transfer in that evaluation, not proof of its cause. Include this failure rather than suppressing it.
4. `LABEL = 1[Corrected_S4 >= 0.20]`, and Corrected_S4 is an input. Directly applying that rule matches the reference exactly by construction. An ML method cannot demonstrate added physical-detection value merely by approximating this same rule. Keep the deterministic rule as a reference-definition baseline, not an independent scientific detector validation.
5. Estimator fitting is normal-only, but development labels are used for AUC-based choices and threshold tuning. Call it **normal-only fitting with label-informed development selection**; do not call the entire workflow fully unsupervised.
6. The historical final period was evaluated previously. New writing, code organization or a fresh fit does not make it untouched. Only genuinely new reserved observations can support the planned independent test.
7. Dates are incomplete within the archive's calendar span; it also contains January 1, 2020. Do not write “a complete year of continuous 2019 coverage.” Verify exposure from the data.

**Mechanistic hypothesis worth testing:** paper-derived boundary settings may be poorly aligned with the rare-reference task. In [OCSVM documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/modules/generated/sklearn.svm.OneClassSVM.html), nu bounds training-error/support-vector fractions; it is not a measured population event prevalence. [Isolation Forest contamination](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.IsolationForest.html) affects its fitted decision offset. The current nu=0.5 and contamination=0.5 settings motivate an ablation, not an assumption that 50% of nature is anomalous. Do not blame every failure on temporal distribution shift until boundary choice, normalization and refit score comparability have been tested separately.

### A falsifiable version of direction A

- **Question:** under what temporal and observation-quality conditions does a high-ranking normal-trained OCC fail to maintain an acceptable reference-alarm burden?
- **Hypotheses:** native boundary settings account for part of the excess alarms; separately fitted score scales contribute to threshold-transfer instability; a simpler calibrated single OCC can match or exceed an ensemble at a declared alarm budget.
- **Falsification:** if prespecified matched-budget comparisons show no reproducible advantage on new data, report that result. If geometry or score-scale diagnostics do not explain errors, do not claim a physical mechanism or distribution-shift explanation.
- **Contribution target:** a verified explanation and transportability test, with a usable decision rule and uncertainty, rather than “we applied three classifiers.” Establish how this extends prior false-alarm/detection literature before writing a novelty claim.
- **Minimum new evidence:** explain the OCSVM threshold failures; test the explanation with controlled ablations; then freeze a primary decision objective and evaluate genuinely new data. Without these, the current study is exploratory evidence for a candidate story, not an established high-impact contribution.

## 6. The story: one question, one defensible answer

Suggested working title for the present scope:

**When high discrimination does not yield reliable alarms: temporal evaluation of rare GNSS S4-reference exceedances**

If future independent event evidence is obtained, revise the title and claim around that validated physical task. Do not use “operationally reliable,” “robust,” “forecasting,” or “state of the art” in the title before the supporting experiment exists.

The argument should follow this chain:

1. **Why the problem matters:** monitoring needs detections at a tolerable false-alarm burden; a flood of alarms or systematic missed episodes can make a statistically impressive score unhelpful.
2. **The precise gap to investigate:** distinguish known general metric limitations from the unresolved, dataset-specific question of boundary/score transport across time and observing conditions. A literature comparison must substantiate the latter gap.
3. **What is tested:** reproduce the paper-derived OCC variants transparently, separate ranking from operating-point behavior, and test a small set of competing explanations.
4. **What is found:** report the actual counts and failures, including simple-model performance. Do not substitute the order in which code cells were executed for the logic of the argument.
5. **What can be concluded:** for current data, retrospective agreement with a derived S4 reference. Broader reliable-monitoring or physical-detection claims require the new evidence above.

A defensible present-tense study synopsis is:

> This retrospective study examines the relationship between score discrimination and decision reliability for rare S4-reference exceedances in GNSS observations. The 2019 benchmark shows that near-unity ROC-AUC can coexist with a large false-alarm burden at the adopted paper threshold. Development-selected operating points produce different detection–false-alarm trade-offs, while threshold transfer fails for some highly ranked models. Because S4 defines the reference and is retained among the predictors, these results evaluate reference agreement rather than independently verified scintillation. They motivate controlled tests of decision-boundary choice and score transport, followed by validation on genuinely new observations.

This paragraph is an honest framing, not an abstract claiming that those future tests succeeded. A stronger contribution sentence must name a demonstrated new finding, its evidence, and its scope. Never replace missing evidence with more elaborate academic language.

## 7. Academic writing rules

The single-contribution/reader-oriented principle is consistent with [Mensh and Kording's writing guidance](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619). The following are project-specific editorial rules, not additional formal journal requirements.

1. **Maintain a claim–evidence ledger.** For every substantive conclusion record the exact table/figure, configuration, denominator, uncertainty and limitation. Mark each statement as observed, inferred, proposed or externally sourced. Never upgrade an interpretation to an observation.
2. **Write for a geophysics reader.** Explain the physical measurement, decision and consequence before introducing the algorithm. Mathematical notation must clarify the task rather than make familiar methods appear novel.
3. **Use one meaning per term.** Distinguish measurement, S4 exceedance, model alarm, reference-positive observation, candidate run and independently adjudicated event. Do not interchange “anomaly,” “scintillation,” “prediction,” “detection” and “forecast.”
4. **Make each paragraph advance the argument.** State its point, present relevant evidence, interpret it within scope, and connect to the next point. Use explicit subjects, concrete verbs and consistent terminology. Precision is more academic than verbosity.
5. **Match verbs to evidence.** “Was associated with” is different from “caused”; “agreed with the reference” is different from “identified real scintillation.” Statistical association, a feature ranking or a SHAP plot does not establish mechanism or causal importance.
6. **Specify comparisons.** “Better” must name the comparator, metric, operating point, dataset/period and uncertainty. Use counts alongside rates; separate percentage-point from relative-percent changes. Do not use “significant” without a justified inferential test.
7. **Avoid promotional claims.** Do not write “groundbreaking,” “best,” “highly accurate,” “robust,” or “generalizable” as substitutes for evidence. Prefer exact observations and qualified conclusions.
8. **Cite the claim actually supported.** Verify authors/year/DOI and read the relevant methods before attributing detailed procedures. Attribute original methods and distinguish the Tsai–Lin adaptation from new contributions. Do not pad references to satisfy an invented minimum or imitate a journal's citations for strategic self-citation.
9. **Preserve negative evidence.** Include zero-detection thresholds, unsuccessful selectors and cases where simpler models win. Report the prespecified family comparisons, not a test-ranked winner presented as preselected.
10. **Use consistent reporting precision.** Counts are integers; rates need denominators; enough decimals to show rare-event differences without suggesting impossible precision. Define S4 and its threshold, score orientation and strict/non-strict decision inequalities explicitly.
11. **Edit in separate passes.** First fix scientific logic/evidence, then section order and paragraph connections, then terminology/grammar, and only then formatting. Fluent prose cannot rescue an unidentifiable claim or a circular validation target.
12. **Human authors own every sentence.** Audit AI-assisted prose for invented mechanisms, stronger causal verbs, fabricated references and unsupported certainty. Record AI assistance according to current publisher policy.

## 9. Scientific reporting and experiment-design safeguards

This section defines standards for approved future analyses. It does not change the current benchmark.

### Measurement and physical interpretation

- Confirm receiver model, signal/frequency, correction implementation and integration interval from original documentation. Distinguish a 60-second reported statistic from 50-Hz raw observations used in other studies.
- Verify whether station coordinates are decimal degrees or another convention, and verify geographic versus corrected geomagnetic coordinates before assigning auroral/polar source regions. The SKN header alone does not establish a plasma-generation mechanism.
- Confirm clock timezone before using UT, local time or magnetic local time. Use “dataset clock hour” until verified; MLT requires a documented transformation.
- Report retained observation time by satellite/condition, data gaps and exclusions. A >60-second lock-time filter can exclude disrupted tracking intervals; investigate censoring before claiming coverage of severe outages. Missing TEC may depend on observing conditions; an imputer does not prove missingness harmless.
- Geometry/CN0/carrier-divergence associations can motivate a nuisance investigation, but multipath, receiver noise and ionospheric irregularities must not be assigned solely from a model's false-positive label.
- Distinguish minute-level exceedances from episodes. Prespecify gap tolerance, satellite matching, station-level merging, episode onset/offset and matching tolerance. An observation-level FP count is not an alarm-episode count or operator workload measure without that definition.

### Validation and selection

- Retain date-grouped, forward-only folds. State that dates may have unequal observed duration; a fold count alone does not describe temporal coverage.
- Fit preprocessing, pseudo-labels, selectors and normalization on eligible training normals only. Document the 5,000-row cap and sampling dates. Never describe K-means pseudo-classes as independently observed scintillation types.
- If a future claim requires unbiased model-selection performance, use a genuinely new final dataset and/or an approved nested temporal selection design. Pooled OOF results used to choose winners are development estimates, not unbiased estimates of those selected winners.
- Do not silently change the current five-fold protocol to nested CV or add a gap. Justify a proposed gap using feature-window overlap, event dependence and the intended deployment setting, then obtain approval.
- Prespecify the primary objective on new development data: for example recall at a defensible alarm budget. Record why that budget is meaningful and whether it refers to per-observation FPR, alarms per exposure-hour, or alarm episodes. A nominal CV_FPR threshold does not guarantee the same future FPR.
- Freeze membership, features, hyperparameters, normalization, score direction, threshold and tie rule before final scoring. Do not select CV_F2 as primary because it happened to have a better historical test F1.
- For any forecast, define t, horizon h and when every feature becomes available. Same-window S4[t+h] cannot be an input for predicting its own exceedance. Include persistence and actual processing latency; a current reference-screening result cannot be renamed forecasting.

### Metrics, uncertainty and fair comparisons

- Report confusion counts, prevalence, PR/AP and ROC-AUC, recall/precision, F1/F2 and relevant false-alarm/exposure rates. Distinguish average precision from trapezoidal PR-AUC. The rationale for emphasizing PR in severe imbalance is discussed by [Saito and Rehmsmeier](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0118432).
- Keep deterministic reference-rule and all-normal baselines visible. Use simple statistical/single-OCC comparators and matched decision costs; more ensemble members are not evidence of better science.
- Use paired temporal/event-block uncertainty when comparing predictions on the same observations. Justify block length and report sparse-positive behavior; millions of correlated satellite rows do not create millions of independent experiments.
- Declare the inferential unit and any multiple-comparison plan before significance testing. Do not run row-wise tests across 65 correlated candidates and present the smallest p-value as confirmatory evidence.
- Define zero-denominator handling and undefined AUC for single-class periods. Do not turn undefined values into perfect or zero performance without a stated policy. Report per-period exposure alongside rates.
- Existing date-block intervals cover count-derived metrics, not automatically ROC/AP or algorithm-selection uncertainty. New intervals/selection corrections require additional analysis; do not claim they already exist.
- Repeated seeds, sample-budget sensitivity and runtime/memory measurements can support stability/efficiency claims. Separate fresh timing from historical cache reuse. “Real-time” additionally requires window availability and measured latency.

These safeguards address known ML-science failure modes; see [Kapoor and Narayanan](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804). The project's particular S4-reference dependency must be stated directly rather than disguised by a generic assertion that no LABEL column was used.

In revision, answer each reviewer point with a verifiable response and a manuscript location; retain unsuccessful or limitation-bearing findings rather than optimizing the narrative to hide them.

## 11. Source maintenance and future Codex behavior
